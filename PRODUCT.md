# Product

## Register

product

## Users

MS Realty serves three connected groups:

- Buyers and renters, often on a phone and sometimes buying from outside Bulgaria, who need to find a suitable property quickly, trust that its facts are current, understand the local process, and contact the agency in their language.
- Sellers and landlords who need a clear valuation and listing path, realistic expectations, and visible follow-through from a local broker.
- Brokers, operations staff, translators, and managers who need a task-first workspace for listings, leads, viewings, follow-ups, documents, translations, migration review, and launch controls without exposing private data or publishing unreviewed work.

The public website supports Bulgarian, English, German, Dutch, Russian, Greek, and Hebrew. The broker workspace supports Bulgarian, Russian, and English. Bulgarian is the source editorial language, and Hebrew must work as a complete right-to-left experience.

## Product Purpose

Turn MS Realty into the operating platform for a family real-estate agency in Sandanski, not a redesigned brochure site. The platform must preserve the search equity of every legacy URL, make property discovery and enquiry fast and trustworthy, give foreign buyers grounded local guidance, and give the agency an auditable CMS and CRM workflow from first contact through viewing, follow-up, and outcome.

Success means buyers can understand and act without friction; brokers can see the next task and complete it in context; every public fact and translation has an accountable human approval; and launch claims are backed by real crawl, review, search, Hermes, and Payload evidence.

## Brand Personality

Warm, grounded, quietly confident. MS Realty should feel like a knowledgeable local who speaks the buyer's language: specific about property facts and process, calm under uncertainty, and direct about what requires human confirmation. The public experience should create trust and momentum. The operator experience should feel focused, familiar, and dependable.

## Anti-references

- Do not resemble a generic property portal built around volume, duplicate inventory, bait prices, or anonymous lead capture.
- Do not resemble brochureware where navigation, decorative sections, or oversized language controls crowd out property search and real tasks.
- Do not make different buyer intents open the same generic modal. Viewing, call-back, enquiry, valuation, saved-search, and language-request flows need intent-specific context and outcomes.
- Do not turn the broker workspace into a wall of empty KPI cards, repeated panels, raw export tables, or desktop-only horizontal scrolling. A screen should lead with the next useful action.
- Do not use display typography, decoration, or excessive card chrome in dense product UI.
- Do not describe Sandanski as a sea destination or imply facts that are absent from approved listing and CMS sources.
- Do not publish Hermes output, translations, customer messages, legal guidance, or property claims without the required human approval.

## Design Principles

1. **The task comes first.** Lead each screen with the search, decision, or next broker action it exists to support; reveal supporting detail when it becomes useful.
2. **Trust is visible.** Show listing freshness, source, reference, review status, responsible person, and process boundaries near the decision they affect.
3. **One intent, one flow.** Carry the user's selected action and property context through an appropriate form, confirmation, CRM record, and follow-up state.
4. **Phone-first without a reduced product.** Reflow navigation, filters, tables, and actions into touch-friendly task surfaces while preserving the full capability set.
5. **Humans remain accountable.** Hermes drafts and assists privately; public and customer-facing changes stay explicit, reviewable, and auditable.
6. **Preserve before expanding.** Legacy URL decisions and source evidence remain first-class operator work until all 457 routes have a deliberate terminal outcome.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA across public and broker surfaces. Use semantic HTML, logical keyboard order, visible focus, error recovery, sufficient text and non-text contrast, 44 by 44 pixel minimum touch targets, reduced-motion alternatives, and status cues that do not rely on colour alone. Public body text must remain readable for older users and in bright mobile conditions. All copy and layouts must tolerate longer translations, and Hebrew must preserve meaning, alignment, icon placement, and interaction order in right-to-left mode.
